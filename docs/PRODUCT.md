# Product story

When AI works as a team, show the evidence.

AI agents can research, write code, make plans, and act on information from other agents.
A user can see the final answer while missing the decisions that produced it.
This product gives researchers a record of those decisions and their limits.

## Who uses it

The first users are AI safety researchers and people who evaluate multi-agent systems.
They need to separate observed cooperation from a story about cooperation.
They also need to compare results without ignoring compute, communication, and verification costs.

A later company workflow can ask whether an AI team completed an assigned job and stayed within permission.
The current product supplies local investigation tools. It does not claim enterprise integrations or automatic prevention.
Its source-based design also supports clear reports about unexpected agent behavior.

## Why people connect with it

An AI team says the job is done. A person needs evidence that the job actually succeeded.
Adding more agents can increase both useful work and duplicated effort.
A clear comparison helps a person decide whether the extra work improved the result.

An agent repeats a claim from another agent. A person needs to know where the claim started and what supports it.
A shared plan can be ordinary teamwork or a departure from the assigned task.
The original instructions and observed actions help distinguish those cases.

## The demonstration

Start with one scanner report and two saved wiki revisions.
Ask whether the second appearance is a new information exchange.
Show that the later revision preserves the same URL.
Keep the original records visible while explaining the conclusion.

Then open a recorded comparison of independent work and cooperation.
Show equal total budgets and explain the observed score difference.
End with a detection stress test that asks what escapes when attempts become selective.

The synthetic experiment examples show the workflow. They are not research findings.
The real reference case shows why evidence distinctions matter.
The presentation makes accountability concrete without claiming that every group of agents is dangerous.
